# ai-governance-toolkit

An AI system register that can be audited, plus the checks an auditor would run on it: EU AI Act
risk triage, a control library mapped to ISO/IEC 42001 themes and the NIST AI RMF, a per-system gap
assessment with an evidence checklist, an application-date timeline for the Act, and runnable
technical checks (drift, fairness with intervals, explainability, data documentation) on a public
credit dataset.

## Why I built this

I spent years auditing models at a bank. The pattern was always the same: a policy said models are
validated and monitored, and when I asked for the evidence it was a person's recollection. An
auditor needs three things for every AI system: it is in a register with an accountable owner, it
was classified for regulatory risk with a recorded rationale, and there is dated evidence that the
controls operate. I wanted each of those to be a file or a command output that can be re-run and
diffed, so the answer does not depend on who is asked.

Not legal advice. The classification engine is a screening aid for an internal register. It applies
a simplified reading of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and may be wrong for your facts. Have qualified
counsel confirm any tier that matters. All register entries are invented and do not describe any
real organisation.

## What is in it

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| `schema.py` | Pydantic v2 models for the register: owner, purpose, users, data categories, model type, vendor or open source, deployment, human oversight, monitoring, last validation, incidents, and the triage questionnaire. JSON Schema is exported to `src/ai_gov/data/inventory.schema.json`. |
| `classify.py` | Ordered rules: Art 5 prohibited practices, Art 6(1) Annex I products, Art 6(2) Annex III with the Art 6(3) derogation and the profiling override, Art 50 transparency, otherwise minimal. Each result carries article references and a rationale. |
| `timeline.py`, `data/ai_act_timeline.yaml` | Application dates by obligation group, original and as amended, and which groups apply to a system on a given date. |
| `controls.py`, `data/controls.yaml` | 22 controls with objective, EU articles, paraphrased ISO/IEC 42001 clause themes, NIST AI RMF function and category. |
| `gap.py` | Selects the controls that apply (tier, personal data, vendor, live status), runs register checks, lists the evidence a person must supply. |
| `evidence/checks.py` | PSI, demographic parity, equal opportunity and disparate impact with bootstrap intervals, intersectional cells, permutation-based explainability coverage, data-dictionary completeness. |
| `incidents.py` | Failure-mode categories mapped to controls; aggregates a user-supplied AI Incident Database snapshot into counts and links. |

## Usage

```bash
python3.12 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e ".[dev,data]"

ai-gov inventory validate          # schema check of the register
ai-gov classify -v                 # EU AI Act tier, references, rationale
ai-gov timeline --as-of 2026-10-28 # which obligation groups apply on a date
ai-gov gap --as-of 2026-10-28      # control gaps per system
ai-gov evidence                    # downloads the UCI data once to data/raw/, runs the checks
ai-gov report --as-of 2026-10-28   # reports/ai_governance_report.md and reports/register.html
ai-gov incidents --snapshot incidents.csv   # optional, counts from your AIID snapshot
```

Use your own register with `--file my_inventory.yaml`. The `--as-of` date decides which obligations
are shown as applicable.

## Results

The committed files in `reports/` are generated by the commands above.

Register triage on the 12 illustrative systems: 1 prohibited (a rejected social-scoring proposal,
Art 5(1)(c)), 3 high-risk (retail credit scorecard, Annex III 5(b); life and health insurance
pricing, 5(c); a vendor CV-screening tool, 4(a)), 2 limited-risk (chatbot and RAG assistant, Art 50),
6 minimal. Card-fraud scoring stays out of high-risk because Annex III 5(b) excludes systems used to
detect financial fraud; motor insurance pricing stays out because 5(c) covers life and health
insurance only. The register checks found 26 control gaps across 8 systems, and 4 live systems have
no validation in the last 12 months.

### EU AI Act dates

Entry into force was 1 August 2024. The table gives the date from which each group applies, and the
original date from Art 113 where Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI) moved it. The
Omnibus was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026
(per two law-firm summaries, see sources). Only adopted law is applied here, not proposals or the
political agreement of 7 May 2026.

| Obligation group | Applies from | Original date |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibited practices (Art 5), AI literacy (Art 4) | 2 Feb 2025 | same |
| Governance, notified bodies, penalties; general-purpose AI models (Chapter V) | 2 Aug 2025 | same |
| Transparency (Art 50) | 2 Aug 2026 | same |
| Art 50(2) marking, generative systems already on the market on 2 Aug 2026 | 2 Dec 2026 | 2 Aug 2026 |
| Added prohibitions (non-consensual intimate imagery, child sexual abuse material) | 2 Dec 2026 | new |
| High-risk, Annex III use cases | 2 Dec 2027 | 2 Aug 2026 |
| High-risk, Annex I products | 2 Aug 2028 | 2 Aug 2027 |

On 28 October 2026 that means, for the register: the three Annex III systems are classified high-risk
but their Chapter III requirements do not yet apply by law, while the prohibition, AI literacy and
Art 50 duties already do. The gap assessment still scores the high-risk controls, because internal
governance does not need to wait for the legal date.

I could not read EUR-Lex from this environment (it answers with a bot check), so I did not verify
the original Art 113 dates or the Omnibus articles against the Official Journal text myself. The
amended dates are cross-checked between the Commission's AI Act Service Desk timeline and the
European Parliament Research Service page; the original dates and the publication dates rest on
the Regulation as I know it and on secondary summaries. Check the consolidated text before
relying on any date.

### Technical evidence on a public dataset

UCI "Default of Credit Card Clients": 30,000 Taiwanese card holders, 22.1% default. Gradient-boosted
trees (scikit-learn), stratified 60/20/20 split, seed 42, SEX excluded from the features. Test AUC
0.786 (95% bootstrap interval 0.771 to 0.800), decision threshold 0.27 (maximum F1 on the validation
set), 23.9% of the test set flagged. This model is a worked example, not one of the register systems.

| Check | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Drift, test vs train | score PSI 0.0017, max feature PSI 0.0034 | pass |
| Drift, simulated shift (aged 40+ vs train) | score PSI 0.0081, but AGE PSI 5.66 and MARRIAGE PSI 0.30 | warn |
| Fairness, SEX | demographic parity difference 0.032 (95% CI 0.010 to 0.052), equal opportunity difference 0.034 (0.002 to 0.087), disparate impact ratio 0.875 (0.806 to 0.960) | pass at the illustrative 0.10 tolerance |
| Fairness, AGE 40+ (proxy) | demographic parity difference 0.040 (0.016 to 0.064), equal opportunity difference 0.016 (0.001 to 0.073), disparate impact ratio 0.849 (0.775 to 0.935) | pass |
| Fairness, SEX x age band | 8 cells of 252 to 1,302 people; demographic parity difference 0.124 (0.084 to 0.182), equal opportunity difference 0.130 (0.101 to 0.277), disparate impact ratio 0.594 | warn |
| Explainability coverage | 8 of 22 features carry 80% of importance (PAY_0 alone 41%); 100% of importance sits on documented features; top-3 local reasons explain at least half of the positive attribution for 98.1% of flagged cases | pass |
| Data documentation | all 25 columns documented, 100% of required fields filled; 8 columns hold values the dictionary does not define (EDUCATION 0/5/6 in 345 rows, MARRIAGE 0 in 54 rows, PAY_x values -2 and 0 in 58% to 72% of rows) | warn |

What the fairness numbers say. Each single-attribute gap passes, and the intervals stay below the
0.10 tolerance. The intersectional view does not: selection rates run from 18.2% for women aged
30 to 39 to 30.6% for women aged 50 and over, and the lowest cell has 59% of the highest cell's
selection rate, under the four-fifths screen. The maximum over eight cells drifts upward through
noise alone, so the interval and the cell sizes matter more than the point value. A single-attribute
pass would have hidden this. Rate gaps are not proof of discrimination; base rates differ by group
and the tolerance is a placeholder the model owner must set. A point estimate inside the tolerance is
reported as warn when its interval reaches past it.

Other results. The shift test is a stress test, not an observed drift: score PSI stayed at 0.008
while the input PSI on AGE was 5.66, so monitoring the score alone would have missed the change.
That is why `MON-01` expects input monitoring too. The PAY_x codes -2 and 0 are widely used in this
dataset but not defined in its published variable description, which the data check reports as a
documentation gap.

## Controls and frameworks

Each control lists the EU AI Act articles, ISO/IEC 42001 clause themes and NIST AI RMF categories it
supports (for example `HUM-01` maps to Art 14 and Art 26, the "use of AI systems" themes, and
GOVERN 3 / MANAGE 1). ISO themes are paraphrased at theme level and no standard text is reproduced;
confirm clause numbering against the purchased standard. The NIST AI RMF is a US government work
and public domain. A gap result of `met` means the register entry is consistent with the control; it
is not an opinion that the control works.

## Incident lessons

[`incident_categories.yaml`](src/ai_gov/data/incident_categories.yaml) maps nine failure modes (bias,
drift, fabricated generative output, privacy exposure, missing human oversight, adversarial misuse,
opaque vendor systems, safety failures, weak incident response) to the controls that would be
expected to catch them. The report includes the mapping table.

There are no incident counts in this repository. On 28 October 2026 the snapshot host listed on the
[AIID snapshots page](https://incidentdatabase.ai/research/snapshots/) timed out on repeated requests,
the GraphQL API accepts only authorised origins, and the project's GitHub repository holds code
without data.

The AIID terms (checked 2026-10-28) license the incident, classification and entity collections of the
snapshots under CC BY-SA but exclude the report text, submissions and media. The repo therefore keeps
counts and incident links only. `ai-gov incidents --snapshot incidents.csv` buckets the title and
description of the `incidents.csv` from a snapshot you download into category counts.

## Quality

```bash
ruff check . && ruff format --check .
pytest -q        # 72 tests, offline, synthetic data only
```

Tests cover schema validation, classification edge cases (fraud carve-out, Art 6(3) derogation and
the profiling override, Annex I conditions, role-specific obligations), the timeline dates and what
applies on a given date, fairness and PSI maths against hand-computed values (and fairlearn when
installed), bootstrap and Wilson intervals, intersectional cell handling, gap logic and the report.
CI runs on Python 3.12 and never downloads the dataset.

## Limitations

- Triage is rule-based on yes/no answers. Real classification needs legal judgement on intended
  purpose, role (provider or deployer) and exceptions. Chapter V general-purpose model duties and the
  Art 111 transitional rules are not modelled.
- Register checks read declared facts. They do not inspect live systems.
- The evidence run covers one public dataset, one model and one random split. The thresholds (PSI
  0.10 / 0.25, 0.10 fairness tolerance, 80% importance mass) are conventions, not standards.
- Explainability coverage uses permutation importance and single-feature occlusion, which are
  approximations of the production model's own explanation method.
